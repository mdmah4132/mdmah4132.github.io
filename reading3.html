Graphic Novel: Motherhood in crisis

After reading the graphic novel "Motherhood in Crisis, " I see that illustrations are one of the strongest ways to tell tragic realities faced by women where giving childbirth is dangerous and nerve racking. I noticed that the visual illustrations showed a different representation in contrast to photographs, and they provide the story with an extra element of emotional depth. The illustrations of the graphic novel are very emotional, and used heavy lines and a wide range of colors to portray the emotional states of the characters. For example, I noticed the facial expressions of women are illustrated with fear, despair, and hope, which enable the viewer to connect emotionally with the struggles of the women in the story. I also noticed, photographs tend to document moments more 'realistically', often failing to show the deeper emotional resonance that illustrations can achieve. For example, It can capture the images of long journeys that women have to make in order to reach the hospital to give birth. I think the illustrations can also be more abstract and symbolic in their interpretation of displaying human experiences of motherhood and survival, which cannot always be represented through photographs. The graphic novel showcases the struggles that the women face at the hands of society and the health system, which highlights the shared struggles that are experienced globally. I believe this distinction is beneficial for us as it allows the reader to feel the emotion of the women and their circumstances, regardless of their gender or nationality. In addition, the illustrations can offer someone a visual representation of the story, which can further embed the narrative in the reader's mind. I noticed this when the character in the story exhibits a feeling of hopelessness, the illustration heightens that feeling for the reader and can add another layer to the story that text alone might not offer. The illustrations can also be a beneficial way of teaching readers about the subject matter of the book.

For instance, when problems occur for a mother’s health and that becomes the subject matter of the book, which can be simplified and made more engaging with illustrated images, and can be popular with people who might not normally be interested in or read reports or other academic journals. And when we combine visual narratives with text-based narratives, I think the book can appeal to a wider range of readers and educate them on the issues presented. As I am someone who visualizes information in a very concrete way, the illustrations add an extra push to my understanding of the stories. It demonstrates a feeling of urgency and a desire to help the women that the book discusses. This is the exact reason why I feel like if one does not get to feel the characters, they won’t truly enjoy reading something in general. So, overall, my take is that illustrations in the graphic novel offer a means of providing the stories with visual content that enhances the emotional depth of the book.
