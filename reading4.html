Artist Talk Sara Stern

I was really interested in Sarah Stern's visiting artist presentation because it changed the way I think about art. I realized that art doesn't just have to be an image or video. I realized artists can tell stories using different forms of media; animation, sculpture, performance, digital imaging, and more. The project I liked the best was Mirror Ball because it used stop-motion animation in a natural setting in Provincetown. I thought it was fun how she used puppets and the nature around her to make something that felt a little bit weird.

The one that I liked the most was "Night Talk," in which she turned a university building into a puppet with a face that could speak to others. This was very interesting to me because she turned an everyday building into a living being and that also sparked the perception of universities, architectures and communities. I learned that even in unexpected ways, art can have a social and political voice.

I enjoyed Sarah sharing the process that she goes through when creating her work, and I was glad that she said how she uses digital methods such as Adobe After Effects to bring her ideas to life. I was also reminded that using technological methods to create work is not always necessary and that I could use material and the environment around me to create work.

In general, this presentation showed me a much different mindset of how I can create my own media projects. I learnt I don't always need to take the usual steps in creating art.
